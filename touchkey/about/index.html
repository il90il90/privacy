TouchKey

An Android app by Israel Cohen · for phones, tablets and Android TV · com.il90.touchkey

What it does

TouchKey lets you decide what a hardware key does. Every key has three slots: a tap, a double tap and a long press. It works with the buttons of the device itself, keyboards, Bluetooth devices, TV remotes and game controllers.

What a key can do

  • Go back, go home, show recent apps, take a screenshot, lock the screen, turn the flashlight on.
  • Open an app, play or pause music, skip a track, change the volume.
  • Press another key, type text, open a web address, send an HTTP request, open an app's activity or send a broadcast, run a shell command.
  • Tap a place on the screen that you point at, or a button found by its name, and wait for that button to appear.
  • Run several steps one after another, or open a quick menu: a wheel of up to eight shortcuts over any screen.
  • Behave differently in an app you choose, while the device is locked, or with the screen off.

How it works

Android shows hardware key presses to an app only through an accessibility service, so TouchKey asks you to turn its service on. It uses it to notice the keys you mapped and the app that is in front, and for nothing else. Tapping on the screen is optional and uses a second, separate service that stays off until you turn it on. An optional Power mode connects the app to the device's own Wireless debugging for actions that need it, such as pressing any key or running a shell command.

Your data

TouchKey has no server, no accounts, no ads and no analytics. Your key setups stay on your device. You can save them to a file, or turn on an optional backup to your own Google Drive, where the app can reach only its own hidden application-data folder.
