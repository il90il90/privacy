TouchKey — Privacy Policy

App package: com.il90.touchkey
Developer: Israel Cohen
Effective date: 24 November 2026

In short
  • TouchKey lets you choose what a hardware key does on a tap, a double tap and a long press: the buttons of your device, a keyboard, a remote or a game controller.
  • We have no server and no accounts and receive none of your data. No ads, no analytics, no crash-reporting service, no tracking.
  • Tapping on the screen for you (a place you picked, or a button found by its name) is optional and uses a second accessibility service that is off until you turn it on.
  • Your key setups stay on your device. They leave it only when you save them to a file, or turn on the optional backup to your own Google Drive, or when an action you made yourself contacts an address you typed.

Who we are

TouchKey is developed and published by Israel Cohen, an individual developer ("we", "us"). We do not operate any server for this app and do not receive any data from it. The only service the app contacts on its own is GitHub, to check for updates (see Checking for updates).

Accessibility services

TouchKey has two accessibility services. They are separate so that remapping keys never needs access to your screen.

TouchKey (keys)

Android only lets an app see hardware key presses through an accessibility service, so TouchKey asks you to turn this service on. It is used for exactly two things:

  • Key presses. It receives hardware key events so it can recognise the keys you mapped and run the action you chose. Keys you did not map are passed on untouched. Key presses are not recorded, stored or sent anywhere. The Key tester screen shows the presses it receives while it is open, and forgets them when you leave it.
  • The app in front. It notes which app is open so that a key can behave differently in an app you chose. Only the app's package name is used, in memory.

This service does not read the content of your screen, your messages, passwords or what you type into other apps, and it does not collect or share any of this.

TouchKey screen actions (optional)

This second service is off unless you turn it on, or, with Power mode on, until a step you set up needs to tap on the screen (for example “Tap a button”, or opening Gemini Live): then TouchKey switches it on by itself so the step can run, and you can switch it off again in Android's Accessibility settings. You only need it if you want a key to tap on the screen for you. It handles no key presses. It is used for exactly two things:

  • Touching the screen. It performs the tap, double tap, long press or swipe at the place you picked.
  • Finding a button by its name. When a key you set up asks for a button, and while you choose a button with “Show the button on the screen”, it reads what Android describes on the screen at that moment: the names, ids and positions of buttons and other items, in whichever app is open. This can include text that is shown on the screen. It is used in memory to find the button and is then discarded.

Nothing this service reads is saved, logged or sent anywhere, with one exception that you make yourself: the name or id of the button you chose, and the app it belongs to, are saved in your setup so the key can find it again. The service does not watch the screen in the background; it looks only at those moments.

Actions you create

A key does what you tell it to. If you create an action that contacts something outside the app, the request goes directly from your device to the destination you entered, and we receive nothing:

  • HTTP request and Open a web address: the address, headers and body you typed are sent to that address when you press the key (or use “Send test”). That service sees your IP address and handles the request under its own privacy policy. Addresses starting with http:// are sent without encryption.
  • Open an app, activity or broadcast, shell command, type text: these run on your device only.

Power mode (optional)

Power mode connects TouchKey to your device's own Wireless debugging so it can press real keys and run shell commands for the actions you made. The connection is from the device to itself; nothing is sent to another device or to us. The local-network and nearby-devices permissions are used only to find and reach that service on the device itself, never for location. If you set up a key for when the screen is off, Power mode listens to hardware key presses while the screen is off, only to recognise that key; these presses are handled in memory and are not saved.

Google Drive backup and Google user data (optional)

You can connect TouchKey to your Google account to back up your setups to your own Google Drive. This is off until you tap “Connect Google Drive” and approve Google's consent screen.

  • What TouchKey can access. Only the permission https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata: its own hidden application-data folder in your Drive. TouchKey cannot see, read, change or delete any other file in your Drive, and it does not access your name, email address, contacts, Gmail, photos or any other Google data.
  • What is stored there. Backup files containing your setups (the items in the table above, except the Power mode pairing key), and with each file: the device model, the name you give the device in TouchKey (it starts as the device's own name from Android's settings, and you can change it), a random identifier created by the app so backups from different devices can be told apart, the TouchKey version, the number of keys, whether the backup was daily or made by hand, a checksum of its contents (so an unchanged setup is not saved twice) and whether you chose to keep it forever. Up to 10 backups per device are kept; older ones are deleted automatically, except the ones you keep forever.
  • How it is used. Only to save your backups, list them by device, show what a backup holds and how it differs from the device in hand, and bring in the one you choose (all of it, or only the keys that are missing) on this device or another one where you connect the same Google account. Every device connected to the same Google account can see the backups and device names of the others. The comparison is done on your device.
  • Who receives it. Nobody but you and Google Drive. The files go directly from your device to Google over an encrypted connection. We have no server and never receive your backups or your Google sign-in. Access is granted to the app on your device by Google Play services; TouchKey does not store your Google password or a long-lived sign-in token.
  • No other use. Google user data is not sold, not shared with third parties, not used for advertising, and not used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models. No person reads it.
  • Deleting it. To delete the backups, open Google Drive on the web › Settings › Manage apps › TouchKey › Options › “Delete hidden app data”. To stop the access, remove TouchKey at myaccount.google.com/connections. You can also turn the daily backup off in the app at any time.

TouchKey's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Voice typing (optional)

If you give a key the “Voice typing” action, pressing it records your voice until the recording ends (a pause, a second press, a time limit or the Done button you chose), turns the speech into text and types the text into the field that has the cursor. While it records, Android shows a “Listening” notification and TouchKey shows a small bubble with the words it hears.

  • The speech is recognised by your device's speech recognition service, which on most devices is provided by Google and may send the audio to Google to turn it into text, under Google's own privacy terms. With “Only on this device” on, TouchKey asks for recognition on the device where the device supports it.
  • TouchKey does not save the recording or the text, and sends neither to us. The text goes into the field you chose; if no field can take it, it is placed on the clipboard so you can paste it.
  • The microphone is used only while a recording you started runs. TouchKey asks for the microphone permission when you save a voice typing action, and you can withdraw it in Android's settings at any time.

Triggers (optional)

A trigger runs the actions you chose when something happens on the device: a Bluetooth device connects or disconnects, Wi-Fi connects or disconnects, the charger is plugged in or out, the battery drops to a level, power saving turns on or off, the screen turns on or off, the phone is unlocked, headphones are plugged in or out, an app opens or closes, a time of day, the device starts, an SMS arrives, a call rings, is missed, answered or ends, an app shows a notification, airplane mode, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi is switched on or off, the ringer mode changes, Do Not Disturb turns on or off, media starts or stops playing (TouchKey only learns that some sound is playing, not what or from which app), or a foldable phone is closed or opened (from its hinge sensor). TouchKey notices these events on the device while its accessibility service runs, keeps only what it needs to see a change (for example the last battery level, or which network was connected), and does not record, send or share them.

A trigger can also have “only when” rules, and any setup can have “If” steps (between some hours, on some days, while charging, on a Wi-Fi network, with the screen on or off, a Bluetooth device connected, an app in front, a setting on or off, the battery level, headphones in, a call going on). They are checked on the device at the moment the trigger would run, from the same state Android reports; nothing about them is kept or sent.

So you can see what your triggers did, TouchKey keeps on the device a short history of each trigger’s last runs (up to 20): when it ran, whether you ran it by hand, and for each of its steps its title, whether it worked and, if not, the reason Android gave. It keeps nothing of what the trigger reacted to (no message text, caller or notification). The history is not sent anywhere or backed up, and is deleted with the trigger.

  • Bluetooth (“Nearby devices”) — asked only when you make a Bluetooth trigger: to list your paired devices so you can choose one, and to know which device connected. The device you choose (its name and address) is saved in your trigger.
  • Location — asked only if you make a Wi-Fi trigger for one named network: Android tells an app a Wi-Fi network's name only with location permission. TouchKey never reads or uses your location. The network name you enter is saved in your trigger.
  • Alarms & reminders (optional) — lets time triggers run at the exact minute.
  • SMS — asked only when you make an SMS trigger: when an SMS arrives, its sender and text are read in memory to see whether a trigger wants it (sender, words, how many in how long). They are not saved, sent or shared, and TouchKey does not read your existing messages.
  • Phone state and call log — asked only when you make a call trigger: to know when a call rings, is answered, missed or ends, and the caller's number (Android gives it only with call-log access). Numbers are compared in memory and not kept. TouchKey does not read your call history.
  • Contacts — TouchKey has no contacts permission. When you choose a person for a trigger, Android's own picker hands TouchKey that one number and name, which are saved in your trigger.
  • Notification access (optional) — only for notification triggers and the “Press a notification button” action, and only if you turn it on: each new notification's app, title and text are checked in memory against your triggers and not kept. While you set up a notification trigger or that action, TouchKey lists the notifications showing at that moment (app, title, text and button names) on that screen, so you can pick one instead of typing; what you pick (the app, some of its words, a button name) is saved in your trigger. When that action runs, TouchKey presses the button you chose on the matching notification, as a tap on it would, and nothing else. The “Reply to a notification” action does the same with a reply button and sends the words you wrote in the action, through that app, as typing them in the notification would. The “Clear notifications” action removes the notifications you chose from the screen; it reads nothing more.

Triggers are part of your setups, so they are included in files you save and in your Google Drive backups.

Checking for updates

TouchKey can look for a newer version of itself on GitHub, where its releases are published (github.com/il90il90/touchkey-releases). When “Check automatically” is on (Settings › About and updates; on by default) it does so once a day and when you open the app (at most every few hours), and also whenever you tap “Check for updates”.

A check is one ordinary web request to github.com. It carries no account, no identifier and nothing about your setups or your device beyond what any web request carries (your IP address and the app name “TouchKey” as the browser name), and it is subject to GitHub's privacy statement. A new version is downloaded only when you tap “Update now”; TouchKey checks that it is TouchKey, newer, unchanged and signed by the same developer. Android then asks you to confirm before it installs; with Power mode on, TouchKey installs it through Power mode instead, without that screen (Android still checks the signature and the version). For that moment the checked file is placed in TouchKey's own folder in shared storage and deleted right after. Turn “Check automatically” off to stop the daily check.

Backup to a file

“Save to a file” writes your setups to the place you choose with Android's own file picker. From then on the file is yours; TouchKey does not keep track of it.

Permissions

  • Accessibility service “TouchKey” — to see hardware key presses and the app in front, as described above.
  • Accessibility service “TouchKey screen actions” (optional) — to touch the screen and to find a button by its name, as described above.
  • See installed apps — to show your apps, with their icons, when you choose an app to open or an app in which a key behaves differently, and to list an app's screens when you build an “activity or broadcast” action. The list is read on the device and is not sent anywhere.
  • Internet, network and Wi-Fi state — for the HTTP and web-address actions you create, the optional Google Drive backup, the update check, and Power mode's connection to the device itself.
  • Microphone (optional) — only for voice typing, while a recording you started runs (see Voice typing). It comes with a foreground service and its “Listening” notification, which Android requires for the microphone.
  • Phone: answer and end calls, make calls (optional) — only for the call steps you set up (“Answer the call”, “End or decline the call”, “Call someone”), and only when that step runs. The number you chose is saved in your setup.
  • Send SMS (optional) — only for the “Send an SMS” step you set up: when it runs, your words go as an ordinary SMS from this phone, through your carrier, to the people you chose. TouchKey sends nothing else and reads no messages for it.
  • Close apps in the background — only for the “Close an app” action you set up, to end the app you chose. It reads nothing from that app.
  • Install apps — only to install a TouchKey update you chose to install; Android asks you to confirm each time, unless Power mode installs it.
  • Local network, nearby Wi-Fi devices (never for location), multicast — Power mode only: to find the device's own Wireless debugging service.
  • Notifications — the box where you type the pairing code while setting up Power mode, a note when a new version of TouchKey is out, and the notifications your own “Show a notification” actions post, with the words you wrote.
  • Run at startup — to start Power mode again after the device restarts (“Start with the phone” in Settings, on unless you turn it off).
  • Ignore battery optimisations — so Android’s battery saving does not put TouchKey to sleep and stop your keys, triggers and timers. Android asks you first; with Power mode TouchKey sets it itself.
  • Wake lock — a few seconds awake to run the action of a key pressed while the screen is off.
  • Vibrate — the optional short buzz when a long press is recognised.
  • Bluetooth (nearby devices), location, alarms & reminders, SMS, phone state, call log, notification access (optional) — only for the triggers you make, as described in Triggers.
  • Do not disturb access, modify system settings (optional) — only for actions you make that switch Do Not Disturb, rotation or brightness, when Power mode is not set up.
  • Permissions given through Power mode — when Power mode is set up, TouchKey can give itself the permissions above (as adb shell pm grant would) instead of showing Android's dialogs: right when a feature you set up needs one, or all at once when you tap “Allow everything at once” in Settings. They are used only as described on this page, and you can take any of them back in Android's app settings.
  • Change secure settings — cannot be granted by tapping; Power mode grants it so TouchKey can switch Wireless debugging back on after a restart and turn its own accessibility service back on if Android switched it off.

The app does not request access to your contacts, camera or photos, and does not read your stored messages or call history. It asks for the microphone only if you use voice typing, for location only if you make a Wi-Fi trigger for a named network (to learn the network's name, never your location), and for SMS, phone and call-log access only if you make an SMS or call trigger.

Children

TouchKey is a general-audience utility and is not directed at children. We collect no data from anyone.

Your choices and rights

We hold no personal data about you, so there is nothing for us to give you, correct or delete. Everything the app keeps is on your device or in your own Google Drive, and you can delete it at any time as described above. Every optional feature (screen actions, Power mode, Google Drive backup, actions that use the internet) can be left off.
